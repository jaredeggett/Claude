The kinetic engine

Your click is the power supply.

Inside every Kinetiq is a miniature electrodynamic generator — the same principle as a bicycle dynamo, shrunk to the size of a fingertip.

  1. 01

    Press

    Your click drives a magnet across a coil, snapping the magnetic field from one state to the other.

  2. 02

    Generate

    That abrupt flux change induces a clean voltage pulse — a few hundred microjoules, harvested instantly.

  3. 03

    Transmit

    The pulse powers a low-energy 2.4 GHz radio that fires your Next / Back command to the receiver.

No supercapacitor top-ups. No sleep-drain. If your finger has energy, so does Kinetiq.

Questions

Frequently asked

So there's really no battery at all?

Correct. Kinetiq has no battery and no rechargeable cell. The mechanical energy of pressing a button is converted into just enough electricity to send the command. If you can click it, it has power.

How can a laser pointer run without a battery?

A small capacitor banks the energy from your clicks. Hold the laser button and Kinetiq spends that kinetic reserve to drive the pointer. A few clicks buys you a burst of laser time — then a few more clicks tops it back up.

Does the receiver need charging?

No. The USB-C nano-receiver draws its trickle of power from the laptop it's plugged into — the presenter unit is the batteryless part.

What software does it work with?

Anything that accepts a standard keyboard clicker: Keynote, PowerPoint, Google Slides, PDF viewers, and most video tools. The receiver enumerates as a plain HID device — no drivers.

What's the range?

About 30 metres line-of-sight on the 2.4 GHz link — comfortably more than any conference stage.
